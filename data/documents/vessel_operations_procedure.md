# Vessel Operations Procedure

*Synthetic/demo document created for a portfolio project. This is not a real operational
manual and does not represent the procedures of any actual company or terminal.*

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure covers terminal-side coordination of vessel berthing, cargo
load/discharge sequencing, and departure for all vessels calling at Terminal A,
Terminal B, or Terminal C. It applies to the vessel planning team, terminal
foremen, and stevedoring coordinators.

## 2. Pre-Arrival Planning

The vessel planning team receives a stowage plan and estimated time of arrival from
the shipping line's agent at least forty-eight hours before a scheduled call. Yard
capacity, crane availability, and labor scheduling are confirmed against the stowage
plan during this window. Any capacity shortfall must be escalated to the terminal
operations manager at least twenty-four hours before arrival so that the shipping
line can be notified of a possible berth delay.

## 3. Berthing Sequence

On arrival, the vessel is guided to its assigned berth by the harbor pilot in
coordination with the terminal's berth planner. Once the vessel is moored and
cleared by port authority, the terminal foreman confirms gangway safety and initiates
the pre-work safety briefing with the stevedoring team before any crane operations
begin. Cranes may not commence work until the vessel is confirmed secured and the
safety briefing is complete.

## 4. Discharge and Load Sequencing

Discharge operations follow the sequence defined in the vessel's stowage plan,
prioritizing containers required for time-sensitive yard moves (such as reefer units
needing immediate power connection) where the stowage plan allows flexibility. Load
operations follow the loading sequence provided by the shipping line, cross-checked
against the yard's container availability and any containers currently on hold per
the Damaged Container Handling Procedure or Delay Management Procedure. Containers
on hold may not be loaded until the hold is cleared.

## 5. Stevedoring Coordination

The terminal foreman coordinates directly with the stevedoring supervisor throughout
vessel operations, tracking crane productivity against the planned schedule.
Productivity shortfalls exceeding fifteen percent against plan for more than two
hours must be reported to the operations manager, who determines whether additional
crane resources can be allocated or whether the shipping line should be notified of a
revised completion estimate.

## 6. Weather and Delay Protocols

Crane operations are suspended when sustained wind speed exceeds the manufacturer's
safe operating threshold for the crane model in use, or during lightning within ten
kilometers of the terminal. The terminal foreman logs the suspension start and end
time, and the vessel planning team assesses impact on the vessel's departure window.
Delays exceeding two hours are logged in accordance with the Delay Management
Procedure.

## 7. Departure Checklist

Before a vessel is cleared to depart, the terminal foreman confirms: all planned
load operations are complete, all lashing and securing work is finished and verified,
outstanding documentation (including any damage or cargo discrepancy reports) has
been transmitted to the shipping line's agent, and port authority departure clearance
has been received. The vessel planning team records final departure time in the
terminal operating system.
