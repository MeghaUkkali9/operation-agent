# Terminal Operations Manual

*Synthetic/demo document created for a portfolio project. This is not a real operational
manual and does not represent the procedures of any actual company or terminal.*

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This manual describes the general operating procedures for container terminal staff,
covering gate operations, yard management, vessel coordination, and shift handover. It
applies to all terminal operators, yard planners, and operations managers working across
Terminal A, Terminal B, and Terminal C. Procedures in this manual are supplemented by
dedicated procedures for damaged container handling, reefer container handling, yard
safety, vessel operations, container inspection, and delay management, which take
precedence in their respective areas.

## 2. Gate Operations

All trucks entering the terminal must stop at the gate control point for credential
verification and container number validation against the terminal operating system
(TOS). Gate clerks confirm that the container number, seal number, and booking
reference match the pre-advised entry. Containers without a matching pre-advice are
held at the gate pending manual verification by the duty operations manager. Trucks
carrying dangerous goods must present a valid dangerous goods declaration before
gate-in is approved. Average gate processing time should not exceed eight minutes per
transaction; transactions exceeding fifteen minutes should be escalated to the gate
supervisor.

## 3. Yard Management

Containers are assigned a yard slot by the yard planning system based on container
type, destination, priority, and expected dwell time. High-priority and urgent
containers are placed in yard blocks closest to the gate and berth to minimize
handling time. Reefer containers are assigned exclusively to powered reefer racks with
active monitoring. Empty containers are segregated from laden containers and stacked
according to the terminal's empty stacking policy. Yard planners re-optimize block
assignments daily based on updated vessel schedules and outbound booking volumes.

## 4. Vessel Coordination Overview

Vessel arrival, berthing, and departure are coordinated between the terminal's vessel
planning team and the shipping line's local agent. Full procedures for berthing
sequence, stevedoring coordination, and load/discharge planning are described in the
Vessel Operations Procedure. This section only covers the operator's responsibility to
confirm that yard capacity and equipment availability are sufficient before a vessel is
accepted onto the berth schedule.

## 5. Shift Handover

At the end of each shift, the outgoing operations manager must complete a shift
handover report covering: open incidents, containers on hold, equipment out of
service, vessels currently working, and any unresolved delay escalations. The
incoming operations manager must review the handover report and confirm receipt
before the outgoing manager leaves the terminal. Handover reports are retained for
thirty days for audit purposes.

## 6. Communication Protocols

Operational communication during active vessel work uses the terminal's assigned VHF
channel. Non-urgent communication is handled through the terminal operating system's
internal messaging module. Any safety-related communication takes priority over all
other traffic and must be acknowledged immediately by the receiving party. Terminal
staff should refer to the Yard Safety Procedure for incident reporting requirements.

## 7. Emergency Contacts and Escalation

Operational issues that cannot be resolved by the duty operator must be escalated to
the shift operations manager. Safety incidents are escalated immediately per the Yard
Safety Procedure regardless of operational impact. Equipment failures affecting active
vessel operations are escalated to the maintenance duty controller. All escalations
outside normal business hours are logged in the terminal operating system with a
timestamp and the name of the escalation recipient.
