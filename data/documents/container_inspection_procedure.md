# Container Inspection Procedure

*Synthetic/demo document created for a portfolio project. This is not a real operational
manual and does not represent the procedures of any actual company or terminal.*

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure defines the inspection steps required at gate-in and prior to vessel
loading to confirm a container's structural integrity, seal status, and documentation
accuracy. It applies to all container types; dangerous goods containers are subject to
the additional requirements in Section 5.

## 2. Gate-In Inspection

Every container entering the terminal is visually inspected by the gate clerk before
acceptance. The inspection covers: exterior structural condition (dents, punctures,
corrosion), door and locking mechanism function, seal presence and seal number match
against the shipping documentation, and confirmation that the container number is
legible and matches the pre-advice. Any structural concern identified at gate-in is
handled under the Damaged Container Handling Procedure rather than this procedure.

## 3. Seal Verification

The seal number on the container must match the number declared in the shipping
instructions exactly. A missing, broken, or mismatched seal is treated as a
documentation discrepancy: the container is held in a designated hold area, the
shipping line and consignee are notified, and the container may not proceed to yard
placement or vessel loading until the discrepancy is resolved and documented in the
terminal operating system.

## 4. Structural Inspection Checklist

Prior to vessel loading, containers that have dwelled in the yard for more than seven
days receive a secondary structural check covering: roof panel condition,
corner casting integrity (critical for safe stacking and lifting), floor condition
for containers that will carry heavy or liquid cargo, and confirmation that no
unauthorized modifications have been made to the container structure. Any container
failing this checklist is reclassified per the Damaged Container Handling Procedure.

## 5. Dangerous Goods Inspection

Containers declared as carrying dangerous goods require additional verification: the
dangerous goods declaration matches the placarding on the container exterior, placards
are undamaged and correctly positioned on all required sides, and segregation
requirements from incompatible dangerous goods classes are checked against current
yard placement before the container is assigned a yard slot. A container with
mismatched placarding or declaration is held and escalated immediately to the duty
operations manager; it may not enter the general yard until resolved.

## 6. Inspection Failure Handling

A container that fails any inspection step is flagged in the terminal operating
system with the specific failure reason, physically tagged, and moved to the
appropriate hold area (documentation hold, damage quarantine, or dangerous goods
hold, as applicable). The operator responsible for the inspection records their name
and the inspection timestamp for audit purposes.

## 7. Recordkeeping

Inspection results, including pass and fail outcomes, are retained in the terminal
operating system for a minimum of one year. Recurring inspection failures associated
with a specific shipping line, container owner, or vessel service should be flagged
to the operations manager for pattern review.
