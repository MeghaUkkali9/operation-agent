# Delay Management Procedure

*Synthetic/demo document created for a portfolio project. This is not a real operational
manual and does not represent the procedures of any actual company or terminal.*

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure defines how terminal staff classify, document, and escalate delays
affecting containers or vessels, and the thresholds at which delays must be
communicated to the shipping line, consignee, or operations management.

## 2. Delay Classification

Delays are classified by cause:

- **Documentation hold**: missing, incomplete, or mismatched shipping documentation,
  including seal discrepancies identified under the Container Inspection Procedure.
- **Customs hold**: a hold placed by customs or another regulatory authority pending
  inspection or clearance, outside the terminal's control.
- **Operational delay**: delay caused by terminal capacity, equipment availability,
  or labor constraints.
- **Weather delay**: delay caused by suspended crane or gate operations due to
  weather, as described in the Vessel Operations Procedure.
- **Damage-related hold**: delay caused by a container being quarantined under the
  Damaged Container Handling Procedure pending disposition.

Every container or vessel delay must be assigned exactly one primary classification
in the terminal operating system, even if multiple factors contributed.

## 3. Documentation Requirements

When a delay is logged, the operator records: container or vessel identifier, delay
classification, date and time the delay began, expected resolution date if known, and
a brief description of the cause. Delay records are updated as new information becomes
available and closed with an actual resolution date once the hold is cleared.

## 4. Notification Thresholds

Containers delayed more than twenty-four hours require notification to the consignee
and shipping line. Containers delayed more than seventy-two hours require escalation
to the operations manager regardless of classification. Customs holds are exempt from
the seventy-two-hour operations manager escalation, since resolution is outside
terminal control, but must still be communicated to the consignee at the
twenty-four-hour mark.

## 5. Escalation Matrix

| Delay duration | Action | Responsible party |
|---|---|---|
| 0–24 hours | Log and monitor | Duty operator |
| 24–72 hours | Notify shipping line and consignee | Duty operator |
| 72+ hours (non-customs) | Escalate to operations manager | Duty operator |
| Any duration, customs hold | Notify consignee only; monitor for status change | Duty operator |
| Any duration, safety-related hold | Escalate immediately per Yard Safety Procedure | Yard safety officer |

## 6. Resolution and Tracking

Once the underlying cause is resolved — documentation corrected, customs clearance
granted, repair completed, or capacity restored — the operator closes the delay
record with the resolution date and a brief resolution note. Operations management
reviews open delays exceeding seventy-two hours on a daily basis to confirm
escalation actions have been taken.

## 7. Recurring Delay Analysis

Delay records are analyzed monthly to identify recurring patterns by cause, terminal,
shipping line, or container type. Terminals with a rising trend in operational delays
should review yard capacity and equipment availability as part of the terminal's
regular planning cycle.
