# Damaged Container Handling Procedure

*Synthetic/demo document created for a portfolio project. This is not a real operational
manual and does not represent the procedures of any actual company or terminal.*

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure defines how terminal staff identify, document, classify, and process
containers found to be damaged during gate-in inspection, yard handling, or vessel
discharge. It applies to all container types except reefer units, which follow the
additional temperature-related steps in the Reefer Container Handling Procedure when
damage affects the refrigeration unit.

## 2. Identifying Damage

Damage may be identified at three points: gate-in inspection (see the Container
Inspection Procedure), yard handling by crane or straddle carrier operators, or vessel
discharge by the ship's crew or terminal foreman. Any staff member who observes visible
damage — including dents, punctures, structural deformation, broken door hinges, or a
compromised seal — must immediately flag the container in the terminal operating system
and physically tag it with a red "DAMAGED — DO NOT MOVE" placard.

## 3. Damage Classification

Damage is classified into three severity levels:

- **Minor**: cosmetic damage that does not affect structural integrity or cargo safety
  (surface scratches, minor dents under 5 cm depth). Container may continue in normal
  circulation after documentation.
- **Moderate**: damage that affects the container's weatherproofing or door
  operation but does not compromise structural integrity (bent door rails, damaged
  gaskets, minor corner casting damage). Container is held pending inspector review.
- **Severe**: damage that compromises structural integrity, cargo safety, or
  stackability (major structural deformation, broken corner castings, punctures larger
  than 10 cm). Container is immediately quarantined and may not be stacked, loaded, or
  discharged until cleared by a certified inspector.

## 4. Quarantine and Documentation

Moderate and severe damage containers are moved to the designated damage quarantine
area within the same terminal. The operator completes a Damage Condition Report in the
terminal operating system, including: container number, damage classification,
photographs from at least four angles, date and time of discovery, and the name of the
staff member who identified the damage. The shipping line and consignee are notified
within four hours of a severe damage classification.

## 5. Disposition Decision

A certified inspector reviews the Damage Condition Report and determines one of three
dispositions: release for normal handling (minor damage confirmed), repair required
(moderate damage, container held until repaired and re-inspected), or condemned
(severe structural damage beyond economical repair). Condemned containers are held for
shipping line disposition instructions and may not be reused for cargo transport.

## 6. Cargo Safety Considerations

If a damaged container contains cargo, the operator must assess whether cargo has been
exposed to weather or is at risk of falling during handling before any further movement.
Containers with exposed or at-risk cargo are handled only under direct supervision of
the duty operations manager, and cargo may be transloaded to a serviceable container if
authorized by the consignee or shipping line.

## 7. Recordkeeping

All Damage Condition Reports are retained for a minimum of two years and are subject to
audit by the shipping line, terminal insurer, or regulatory authority. Repeated damage
patterns associated with a specific vessel, stevedore team, or yard equipment unit
should be reported to the operations manager for root-cause review.
